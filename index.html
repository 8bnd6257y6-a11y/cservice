Three disciplines that carry a wine from the press to the shelf — each one built on the previous.

01

Varietal enology

Enology is varietal when it approaches the raw material conservatively. Knowing what a cultivar can do lets us plan vinification protocols in detail, so that the character of each grape is amplified rather than corrected.

Protocols by objective

  • Novello wines by carbonic maceration
  • Aged red wines
  • Varietal and aromatic white wines
  • Aged white wines
  • Sparkling and semi-sparkling wines
  • Wines from dried and late-harvested grapes

Quality management

Markets and modern distribution follow the rules of a globalised world. Meeting voluntary and mandatory international standards is no longer optional — it is the price of entry.

04Viticulture services

The vineyard is where quality is decided.

Management, technology and design — three ways of working on the same hectare.

01

Vineyard management & varietal expression

That quality is made in the vineyard is now widely accepted. Only attentive management that integrates every aspect of the estate delivers appreciable results. From winter pruning onwards the qualitative outcome can be directed with the right techniques — including those that extend the working life of the vineyard.

Systemic diseases such as esca cause sudden dieback; correct pruning practice, both winter and green, helps keep the situation under control. Green operations become a powerful tool in the grower's hands, but only a deep knowledge of the physiology behind the biological processes can direct those choices properly.

Areas of consultancy

  • Soil management
  • Correct pruning operations
  • Fertilisation management and optimisation
  • Canopy management for varietal expression
  • Integrated pest management and organic regimes
  • Physiological and precision irrigation programmes
02

Precision viticulture

By interpreting multispectral remotely sensed data we build prescription maps that optimise mechanised and manual operations in the vineyard. The same reading supports decisions at planting — rootstock choice and detailed water regimes.

At harvest, a detailed view in multispectral bands makes it possible to differentiate the crop and rationalise winemaking interventions.

Quality is made of details

  • Precision farming
  • Remote sensing
  • Prescription maps
  • Variable-rate application
  • Precision irrigation
03

Vineyard design

The birth of a great wine should begin with the design of the vineyard. This phase must be studied in every detail, starting from the site — pedology and climate — adapting trellising to the choice of material, clone and suitable rootstock, and using healthy propagation material.

At this stage it is also possible to design systems suited to high mechanisation, with innovative irrigation systems for every production requirement.

Main topics

  • Planting density
  • Planting material
  • Rootstock and clone selection
  • Drainage lines
  • Irrigation systems
  • Design for different levels of mechanisation
